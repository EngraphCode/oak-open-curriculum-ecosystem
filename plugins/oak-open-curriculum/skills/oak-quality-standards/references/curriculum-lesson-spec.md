# Annex B — Curriculum and Lesson Specification

Oak's specification for curricula and lesson resources — the expectations the quality standards operationalise. Use it for the intent behind a standard and for the spec-level requirements (counts, formats, structure). Reproduced from Annex B (the table of contents, version history, and footnotes are omitted).

## Context

It is the teacher, not the resources, that teaches. Oak's resources are adaptable and optional — a starting point teachers use flexibly. Consistency is offered for: **accessibility** (all pupils can access content; international standards), **technical standards** (standard tools/formats for a single platform), **quality standards** (evidence-based approaches), and **familiarity** (a consistent style). The specification cannot account for every subject/phase variation.

## Curriculum

1. Designed and developed by experts with both subject and curriculum expertise.
2. Curriculum intent is clearly defined, articulated and evident.
3. Content is broken down into units.
4. Units are sequenced thoughtfully and cumulatively across the phase, considering prior knowledge.
5. Threads group and link conceptually connected units, within and across year groups and phases.
6. Units associated with each thread are purposefully and coherently linked to build knowledge, skills and understanding over time.
7. Where appropriate, units are identified that users could adapt to local context without compromising the sequence.
8. Vocabulary is mapped across units and lessons.
9. Independence increases over time by gradually reducing scaffolding.
10. Challenge increases throughout so the curriculum functions as a progression model.
11. All content aligns to the subject-specific frameworks (Annex A).
12. All content meets Oak's Detailed Lesson Criteria.
13. Supporting content is drawn from a diverse range of sources, authors, composers and speakers.

### Units

- Each unit has a unique **unit title** reflecting its content.
- A list of **five to ten prior knowledge requirements** most important for accessing the unit.
- For each unit, one **link to a prior unit** and one **link to a future unit**, identified and explained.
- A list of **National Curriculum content points** covered.
- For Key Stage 4, a list of **exam board specification content** covered.

### Lessons

- Lessons within a unit form a coherent whole, sequenced so content builds cumulatively.
- Each lesson has a unique **lesson title** reflecting its content.
- A one-sentence **pupil lesson outcome**.
- Up to five **key learning points** (new knowledge).
- Each lesson is composed of two to four **sections** forming the **lesson outline**.
- A list of **copyrighted content** requirements essential to the outcome.

## Oak lessons (general)

1. Planned and designed by expert teachers.
2. Suitable both for in-school delivery and remote learning.
3. Broken into sections; in most cases each section is a cycle of explanation, checks for understanding, practice and feedback.
4. Planned to last ~50 minutes at KS2–4 and ~40 minutes at KS1.
5. Resources complement each other but can also be used independently.
6. All safety and safeguarding risks addressed, mitigated and highlighted.
7. Scaffolding ensures all pupils can access learning.
8. Vocabulary is explicitly taught before it is required.
9. Oracy is developed throughout all subjects.
10. Spelling, punctuation and grammar are correct and consistent.
11. Lessons can be adapted by the end user; text must be editable, not in an image.
12. Content is age appropriate throughout.
13. Content makes pupils think hard.
14. Unfamiliar, non-essential vocabulary is defined in the moment.
15. Where live modelling is appropriate, content to be modelled is in the deck and modelled in the video.
16. All facts, figures and calculations are accurate.
17. Comments and notes are removed before publishing.
18. Equations are created using software specified by Oak.

### Accessibility

1. Content is created and designed to be accessible to all teachers and pupils.
2. Content enables the **Web Content Accessibility Guidelines v2.1 AA** standard to be met.
3. Colour is not used to convey meaning unless essential, and is dual-coded where possible; written references refer to both codings.

### Lesson data

Up to five **keywords** with definitions; a **misconception or common mistake** with a way to address it; **content guidance** for sensitive/safeguarding content; the teacher's name, bio and headshot; all **equipment and resources**; supplementary media in high-quality format logged via Oak's Digital Asset Management; details of any third-party copyright content; one **teacher tip** per lesson.

## Slide deck

- Every lesson includes a downloadable slide deck, suitable for in-class use and the video, aligned to Oak's template.
- Divided into sections; a title slide aligned to the subject-specific template; a lesson outline slide; keyword slides; a lesson summary slide at the end.
- Checks for understanding directly follow explanations and assess the key learning points.
- Tasks and answers are included and match the worksheet.
- Provides the necessary illustrative content; manages cognitive load; conveys complex ideas simply; misconception content is explicit; model responses are given where there may be no single correct answer.
- Technical: 16:9 Google Slides; each deck ≤ 10mb as .pptx (note: a later standard sets 20mb — defer to the current standard); supplementary audio/video uploaded as .mp3/.mp4, not embedded.

## Video

- Planned and filmed by expert teachers who convey enthusiasm.
- Covers the same content as the deck; guides pupils through a remote lesson while remaining useful to teachers.
- Teacher head-and-shoulders shown when talking (full screen or top-right); appropriate, conversational, supportive tone; gestures support understanding.
- Practical activities/demonstrations/modelling are part of the video, filmed for use in or out of context, following health-and-safety best practice.
- Oral explanation complements the slides, is clear and concise (best achieved through scripting), conveys complex ideas simply, offers active participation, motivates.
- Pupils are told to pause with at least 5 seconds of silence for CfUs; encouraged to complete the exit quiz.
- Technical: ideally 25–30 min (strict 20–35); .mp4; 16:9, min 1920×1080 (1080p); audio ≥ 192kbps AAC; clear synchronised visuals.

## Worksheets

- An Oak **worksheet** collates the lesson's practice tasks (which may or may not need a written response).
- Every lesson has a worksheet; split into tasks that match the deck.
- Where there are written tasks, a separate **worksheet answers** document includes all written tasks with correct answers.
- Aligned to Oak's template; task types varied and suitable for subject/phase; two versions of a task allowed for in-school vs remote.
- Tasks can be completed independently; assess associated key learning points; make pupils think hard.
- Technical: A4 portrait Google Slides; no audio/video; each task's instructions fit one slide; each page 1–3 tasks; 2–4 tasks per lesson; ≤ 10mb as .pptx; space for answers.

## Quizzes

- Every lesson has a **Starter Quiz** (assesses prior knowledge) and an **Exit Quiz** (assesses the lesson's learning).
- Completing the Exit Quiz correctly means the key learning points are understood.
- Each quiz has exactly six questions (note: PE and some subjects set four — defer to the subject's current standard); questions are unique within the subject phase.
- Each question has one piece of relevant feedback (correct answer + reason/explanation/calculation) and one relevant hint.
- Question types: multiple choice, short answer, or sort (order/match).
- Types varied across a unit; challenge increases slowly with question number; answerable independently of the lesson; at least one question tests vocabulary; starter questions assess prior (not to-be-taught) knowledge and engineer early success (>75% expected) while still requiring thought; images include alt text.
- Authored using Oak's internal quiz authoring system.

## Additional materials

- For teacher-facing material beyond the above (e.g. science practical guidance; long text extracts).
- Aligned to Oak's templates; produced as Google Docs; ≤ 10mb as .docx.

## Appendix A — task types

A non-exhaustive set; vary task types within lessons and across units: **example analysis**, **complete it**, **facts/figures/definitions**, **copy me**, **practise a skill**, **where do these belong?** (classification/ordering/matching), **apply this to…** (a new context), **form a judgement**, **investigate this…**.

## Appendix B — question types

- **Multiple choice** — a stem then answer choices; one or two correct answers and up to five distractors; choices may have images.
- **Short answer** — a stem with an answer input, out-of-line (after the stem) or in-line (within it); several acceptable answers possible.
- **Sort (order/match)** — order questions sort items into a specified order (3–7 choices); match questions pair against a fixed list (3–6 pairs, equal numbers of choices and matches).

## Appendix C — science practical guidance

Each practical activity needs a Practical Guidance document: a unique **title**; the **purpose** (with substantive knowledge needed and exam-board apparatus/technique codes); **materials** per pupil/group and for the teacher; **technical guidance** (preparation, adaptations, risk assessments from Oak-approved bodies); a numbered **method**; and **sample data** where results may be inaccurate.
