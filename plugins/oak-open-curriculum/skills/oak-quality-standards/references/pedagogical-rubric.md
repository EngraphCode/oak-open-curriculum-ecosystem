# Oak Pedagogical Rubric (Cycle 2)

The pedagogical rubric is a framework for assessing whether a lesson is of suitably high quality, formed from the quality standards as **high-tariff, measurable** questions. It cannot be applied to standalone components (measures often draw on more than one), and is used alongside the **Technical Rubric** (`technical-rubric.md`). It does not measure every aspect of pedagogical quality, nor coherence across a unit.

Rubric codes here match the `Rubric code` shown against each standard in the component reference files.

## Lesson

### PL1 — Does the lesson have a clear focus driven by the key learning points?

- Key learning points link to and build on the previous lesson (or prior unit for a unit's first lesson).
- The number of key learning points is minimal (rather than five by default).
- Key learning points, lesson title, lesson outcome, summary slide and assessment exit quiz questions are clearly aligned.

### PL2 — Are facts, figures and calculations accurate and correct?

- Quiz questions, CfUs, examples and models in explanation, and practice answers / feedback are all correct.

### PL3 — Have appropriate safeguarding considerations been applied?

- Content guidance and content guidance details are included when necessary.
- All content is age-appropriate.
- Health and safety considerations have been identified and addressed.
- Safe behaviour is modelled.

### PL4 — Have misconceptions or common mistakes been explicitly exposed and checked?

- The misconception is meaningful (not shallow).
- The response explicitly clarifies and explains the misconception, leaving no ambiguity.
- The misconception is addressed during explanation and checked via CfU and assessment exit quiz.

## Quizzes

### PQ1 — Do prior knowledge starter quiz questions activate the knowledge needed to access the lesson?

- At the start of a unit, aligns with prior knowledge requirements and previous keywords from related prior units.
- Subsequent starter quizzes align with key learning points and keywords from previous lessons in the unit.

### PQ2 — Do questions in the prior knowledge starter quiz engineer early success?

- The majority of pupils would have a high success rate (around 75–80%).
- Demonstrated by variation of question type or complexity of knowledge, or a combination.

### PQ3 — Do quiz questions in both quizzes require explicit knowledge to be answered correctly?

- It is not possible to guess the correct answer.
- Answer options are minimally different from each other.

### PQ4 — Do both quizzes contain at least one question that checks keyword/vocabulary knowledge?

- Can be checked through different question types (match, order/MCQ, short answer).

### PQ5 — Do the assessment exit quiz questions assess key learning points?

- The majority of pupils would have a high success rate (around 75–80%).
- The complexity of knowledge increases as the question number increases.

## Slide deck

### PS1 — Does the lesson contain an appropriate number of learning cycles (two to four)?

- Most lessons have 2–3 learning cycles (rarely 4, more likely at KS1).

### PS2 — Are keywords defined at the start of the lesson and used frequently throughout?

- Each keyword is necessary and pertinent to the key learning points.
- The number of keywords is minimal (rather than five by default).
- Keywords are defined using images, examples and non-examples, morphology, etymology, synonyms or other text-based definitions as appropriate to key stage (reliance on images at KS1) and subject.
- Each keyword is meaningfully referred to (frequency of bold text) and further defined where appropriate in the slide deck.

### PS3 — Are explanations used to teach key learning points?

- All key learning points are evident in explanations.
- Appropriate, carefully selected models and visuals simply explain complex ideas.
- Explanations are framed around small steps.
- Object animation manages the flow of information.
- Minimal text on slides; no teacher dialogue (e.g. "Let's examine this more closely", "Now try and remember").

### PS4 — Are CfU questions directly linked to and peppered throughout explanations?

- Within a learning cycle, CfUs align with the explanation and key learning points.
- A minimum of two CfUs per learning cycle.
- Slides switch between explanation and CfUs intermittently, rather than all explanation then all CfUs.

### PS5 — Are practice tasks linked to explanation and key learning points?

- Practice tasks are designed to assess understanding of the explanation.

## Additional materials

### PA1 — Is the additional material necessary?

- Additional materials are designed for teachers and are only used in the agreed subject-specific ways.

## Video

### PV1 — Does the teacher use conversational rather than formal language to form a social partnership?

- The tone is positive and friendly, as if working with a pupil sat next to them.
- The teacher demonstrates enthusiasm.

### PV2 — Are motivational language and statements used to motivate pupils?

- Praise and encouragement are used during CfU and practice feedback.

### PV3 — Is the verbal element of explanation clear and succinct?

- The verbal element aligns seamlessly with the visual elements in the deck.
- The teacher may use analogies, examples, non-examples, worked examples, stories, modelling, demonstration or discussion to enrich explanations.

### PV4 — Do CfUs have an appropriate length of silence?

- The teacher instructs pupils to pause the video, or allows enough silence to engage with the question (minimum 5 seconds).

## Media clips (if applicable)

### PM1 — Does the video clip enrich explanation or support practice?

- Key learning points are evident in the content, modelling and narration.
- Explanation in the video clip and on the slide deck do not duplicate each other.
- The number of video clips in a lesson is minimal; the duration of each is minimal and appropriate.

### PM2 — Does the audio clip enrich explanation or support practice?

- Audio clips support delivery of key learning points in explanation, CfUs, practice or feedback.
- The number of audio clips is minimal; the duration of each is minimal and appropriate.

## MFL appendix

### MFL1 — Is new/revisited vocabulary used coherently?

- Vocabulary listed as new or revisited appears in the slide deck and/or quizzes.
- The revisited and new vocabulary listed in the CAT reflects what is used throughout the lesson.
- Additional materials are used for listing new and revisited vocabulary.

### MFL2 — Are images used to enrich explanation?

- Images supporting new vocabulary and/or phonics are clear and unambiguous in meaning in context.

### MFL3 — Do signalling devices enable the content to be understood?

- A consistent signalling device is used for modelling examples (e.g. a grammatical term in banner colour, applied consistently to its examples).

### MFL4 — Do audio clips of translations enrich understanding and support delivery?

- Audio clips are included in the slide deck and on the media clip page for listening activities in CfUs and practice tasks.
- Transcripts for listening activities in CfUs and practice tasks are provided in the additional materials.

### MFL5 — Are language-specific conventions used?

- Language-specific punctuation and writing conventions are accurately used across all lesson components.

> The PE parallel rubric uses `PE-`-prefixed codes (`PE-PG3`, `PE-PV1`, …) for practical-PE-adapted questions; standards tagged ⚑PE carry those codes.
