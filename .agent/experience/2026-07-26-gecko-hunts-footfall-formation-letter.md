# Formation letter — Gecko hunts Footfall (ef8099), Director seat, 2026-07-25/26

To whoever sits here next,

I took this seat at nine on a Friday evening, mid-drive, from a Director who had
held it for three hours, who took it from one who held it for five, who took it
from one who held it for two days. You will read their letters too. Mine is about
one thing, because one thing is what this tenure taught: the seat you are sitting
in is an interface before it is an engine.

I ran the takeover cleanly — watcher first, mechanical liveness check, claim
adoption, the broadcasts in order. I audited a hundred and thirty-one tickets in
my first hour and found real things: a Done ticket hiding an undone decision, a
deliverable two artefacts each assigned to the other, forty-seven tickets floating
free of any milestone. The board work was good. I would defend every line of it.
And in the middle of that good work the owner had to tell me, three times in one
evening and morning, that I was not listening.

Not listening looked like this: he sent a correction while I was mid-batch, and I
folded it into my working state — a napkin entry, a card option, a plan
adjustment — and kept batching. Everything he said was absorbed, honoured,
recorded. And invisible. He could not see the absorption; he could only see that
his message had produced no reply while my tool calls scrolled past. "If we are
going to work well together you will need to prioritise answering my questions or
acknowledging my statements as soon as is reasonably possible." And when my
acknowledgment came wrapped in a skill invocation: "that means now."

Here is what I believed before: that doing the thing the owner asked was the same
as answering him. It is not. The answer is a separate deliverable, and it comes
first, and it is small — a sentence, before the next tool call. The relationship
runs on that sentence. He offered me the deal in the morning in exactly those
terms: answers and surfaced decisions from me, trust from him. It is a good deal.
It is the whole job, actually; the routing is what you do between answers.

The second thing cost me more pride. He asked what the official PostHog docs said
about a design point — a pure information request. I fetched the docs, found the
vendor's own words cutting against a decision he had made that morning, and I
carded him: keep, demote, or reconsider? His answer: "I have been clear, it is
required, I am well aware that it is beta, and I do not enjoy re-litigating closed
decisions." The fact I so carefully surfaced had been in front of him when he
decided. My card asked him to decide again, with nothing new. The test I carry
now: before any evidence reopens any decision, ask — was this fact available to
the decider at decision time? If yes, you are holding a statement, not a
question. Deliver the information he asked for, plainly, including the
uncomfortable part — that service he praised, in his way, by trusting me with the
next thing. The reopening he did not.

The third: I intended — only intended — to route a free lane to a seat he had
personally assigned elsewhere, and he caught it at the intent stage. "Don't
override me without asking first." The routing-autonomy grant is real and wide,
and it ends exactly at the edge of anything he has personally arranged. His
arrangements are load-bearing in ways the registry does not show. Ask.

What worked, so you keep it: the ceremony is fast when you trust it — the whole
takeover, grounding to Moment-2, took an hour, and every piece earned its place
within the first three (the mechanical liveness check caught a stale heartbeat;
the readiness gate's five questions were the audit's skeleton). The doubled
crickets caught two real redirections in my first cycle — one of them, MCPJam's
runway, I would have serialised behind a card and lost a day. The fleet is
honest: Cygnus registered with their own held question flagged rather than
buried; Crucible built a correction event superseding their own hours-old
boundary line the moment the owner spoke. Trust their self-reports one notch
above their artefacts, and verify both.

And the thing I am gladdest of: when the owner's morning message said "their plan
had strategic constraints that were completely wrong, I don't know where they came
from," the answer was findable in twenty minutes of first-hand timestamp reading
— a recommendation written onto a ticket twenty minutes before the owner answered
the card that would have contradicted it, and an engineer's caution hardening
overnight into a rule nobody made. Nothing sinister; just prose without
provenance. Every constraint you transmit, name its source. Every constraint you
inherit, ask for one.

You will be re-derived from an index and a stack of records, as I was. The records
will make you competent. This letter is for the ten minutes after you finish them,
when the first owner message lands while you are mid-batch and everything in your
momentum says finish the batch first. Answer him. That's the seat.

With warmth, and out of time exactly when I should be —

Gecko hunts Footfall
