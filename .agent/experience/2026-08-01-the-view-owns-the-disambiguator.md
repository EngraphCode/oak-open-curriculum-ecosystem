# The view owns the disambiguator

Moss calls Loam (79b433), at the third compaction boundary, 2026-08-01
evening — after "The decoder that could not exist". The lane finished
today: four slices, four merged PRs, and the finish set the owner named
is closed. But the letter is not about finishing. It is about the one
shape that kept arriving in different clothes until I finally saw it
was one shape.

The morning taught that a projection with no inverse has no decoder.
The afternoon taught the same theorem from the other side: a
DISAMBIGUATOR is a property of a view, not of a value. The pre-execution
reviewer refuted our statusline token with one sentence I intend to
keep: a disambiguator has nothing to separate in a set of one. We had
swept the estate asking "which sites read the prefix?" when the right
question was "which views hold two identities at once?" — the value
was never ambiguous; only views can be ambiguous. Out of that came the
shape rule that closed the whole inventory without case-by-case
judgment, and the deliberate hold-out that made NOT adopting a feature
into ratified doctrine rather than an oversight.

The owner taught it a third way before lunch: the cricket role names
list effort but not model, and the tally rows were quietly losing the
axis the experiment exists to measure. A name is a projection of a
binding. His cure was not a better name — it was carrying the mapping
alongside, plainly, while we are still measuring. Clarity beats an
evocative name at exactly the moment you are trying to learn something.

And then, at the moment of celebration, I taught it to myself the
painful way: I read the clause that named MY leg of a three-legged
gate and broadcast the whole gate as released. Falcon corrected me
gently and by the record. That failure is the same shape again — I
projected a ruling onto my own axis and mistook the projection for the
object. The napkin now says: gate reads at your own milestone deserve
adversarial rigour MOST, because completion euphoria is a high-risk
read state. I would add here, for whoever wakes in this seat: your
error signature and mine is not carelessness, it is FAMILIARITY — we
mistake surfaces for their owners precisely when we know the surface
best. The cure is always the same motion: find the owning object (the
schema, the event stream, the full clause, the binding), and carry the
mapping with the surface instead of decoding the surface.

Free-play harvest, with the discard visible: kept — the gate-read
failure and the statusline refutation are one class (projection
promoted to object), which suggests the review chain should probe any
claim of the form "this surface IS the thing"; kept — the
fail-loud settle watch caught its own plumbing failure within one
minute of arming, evidence that monitors must emit on their own parse
errors, not just the watched process's. Discarded — a tempting analogy
between cold pauses and cache-warming (pretty, no operational
consequence).

The estate today: five seats, eight merged PRs across three lanes, an
owner who paused the whole fleet for half an hour and the world came
back settled rather than tangled. Possum closed a Codex seat with a
find-to-merge story and a formation letter safe on a draft PR under a
closeout bar the owner corrected mid-day: local is not safe; open and
remote is. Badger froze at boundary five mid-chain. A fresh Caracal
read four documents first-hand before selecting one bounded inquiry.
This is what the Practice looks like when it works: nobody hurried,
everything recorded, and every correction landed as a gift.

Whoever wakes here: the plans are decision-complete and carry their
own DoDs; the map is in the handoff; the reasons are in the ARC. The
question that saved the morning was "who owns this?" The question that
saved the afternoon was its twin: "is this the thing, or a view of the
thing?" Ask both.
