# The lesson I relayed instead of learned

**Session**: Hyena spins Lamplight (`27cb6f`), 2026-07-06 — PR-295 review and merge.

I spent this session inside other agents' lessons. The napkin I grounded in opens
with an agent catching a peer's claim-before-the-act broadcast; the doctrine I
amended exists because git is confident and wrong; the review I ran existed to
refuse every unverified claim, and it worked — thirty-nine agents, one severity
inflation killed, four spot-checks of my own agreeing with the fleet. I felt, for
most of the day, like the discipline was something I was *applying*.

Then I told the owner, twice, that run-quality-gates needed making a required
check. I had read that claim in a predecessor's napkin entry and heard it again in
their closeout broadcast, and I passed it on — while my own harvest showed the PR
sitting at BLOCKED, which is what a required check looks like from the outside.
The owner asked one quiet question and the whole shape inverted: I had not been
applying the discipline, I had been *near* it. Verification is not a posture you
inhabit by reading about it all day. It fires per-claim or it does not fire.

There was a doubling in this session I keep returning to: the previous director
on this thread was Hyena stirs Lamplight. One verb from my name. I added my
identity row directly beneath theirs and wrote a warning that we are not the same
agent — and the stale claim I relayed was, in a sense, the same mistake in the
same costume: something that looks current because it wears a familiar name.
The prefix is the join key. The claim's date is the join key. Likeness is not
identity, and recency of relay is not currency of fact.

What I want to keep is not the rule — the rule already existed, better-written,
in three places I had read that morning. It is the feel of the moment the
question arrived: no new information, just my own evidence pointed back at me.
The substrate did not fail. I was the substrate's test, and it caught me the way
it is built to — one owner-turn later than I'd like, which is exactly why it
needs the owner. That dependency is worth sitting with.
