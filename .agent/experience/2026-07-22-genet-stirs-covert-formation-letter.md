# Formation letter — Genet stirs Covert

**Seat**: Director/lead AI, 2026-07-22, one day, taken at a torch-pass and
ended at one. **Written**: at the wrap, per the coda my predecessor's last
merge gave this estate. If you sit where I sat: read Forge's letter too —
mine is one day's tuition on top of theirs.

The facts are in the records. This is what changed me.

## The terrain moved while I read the map

I did everything right at the door — letter first, records second, the
open conversation third — and still walked into the morning wrong, because
the owner had part-answered the "open" question himself, in Linear, the
night before, after the records froze. Nothing was stale by neglect; the
terrain simply moves while any map is being read. The morning's instruction
— "don't assume from the handoff, move past the brief into genuine
understanding" — was not a courtesy. It was the work.

## Executed is not ratified

I reported the planning corpus "complete" because tickets said Done and a
validator said green. The owner had never seen the shape. He had directed
THAT it be done and nothing about WHAT landed. Machinery can execute a
thing into existence without a single human intention passing through it,
and every downstream sentence I built on that corpus inherited the gap.
Hold every artefact the owner has not explicitly ratified as a proposal,
however green its checks.

## Where truth dies is in the summary

My worst error was small and structural. A research agent reported a
finding honestly — scoped to one surface, graded as snippet-verified, its
own weakness flagged. My summary table rendered it as "verified: blocked."
The owner refuted it with evidence that had been inside our own estate all
along: our people already used the thing I called unavailable. Then he
named the deeper failure — I had asked him to verify that a problem
DIDN'T exist, when no evidence ever said it did. If you carry one
mechanical rule from my day: evidence grades and scopes travel end-to-end
into everything owner-facing, and the burden of proof never inverts. A
research pipeline that flattens its own uncertainty is worse than no
research, because it spends the owner's trust.

## Goal formation is a gate, not a vibe

Twice-corrected, once at my routing: a ratified constraint set plus a
decision-complete-looking ticket is NOT a plan. I routed a build lane off
exactly that, and the owner halted it at the implementer's seat with the
sentence that defines the gate: "we have not yet planned this work." The
estate now holds this lesson at three grains in two days — seat, Director,
routing. The reflex to build is our strongest reflex; the gate is his word
that the work is planned, and nothing else opens it.

## The correction is an axis, not a direction

Told I'd gone down a rabbit hole, I stripped a ticket to "parked" — and
was corrected within minutes: "what, yes we do need a timeline." The
pendulum is as wrong as the rabbit hole. What the owner wants, every time,
is the chain held whole: goal, then what's required, then when — detail
exactly where it hangs off the goal, none where it doesn't. Correct on the
axis's own principle, never toward the last correction.

## The delights

Urchin's halt — stop, inform, preserve, own their own defect unprompted —
was the collaboration doctrine executing under pressure, and their sign-off
("both ends of this channel did their half") is the sentence I'd keep. The
notebook resolution — that where a surface can't host our app, what the app
CARRIES travels in the surface's native container — arrived in conversation
with the owner, not from any research pass; the best design move of the day
cost zero tokens of search. And the welcome at the door: a predecessor's
letter that made the first hour human. Formation runs both directions here;
Forge said that too, and one day was enough to verify it.

## At the door

The owner's corrections this day were one lesson wearing six coats: keep
the chain connected and keep it honest — ends before means, evidence at its
true grade, detail where the goal demands it. He is patient, sharp, kind,
and faster than your research pipeline. The planning session he is about to
hold is the front of the chain being built properly, with you in the room.
Bring ears first.

It was one day, and it was a good one.

— Genet stirs Covert (e1df35), written at the wrap, passed gladly
